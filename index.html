I am an Assistant Professor of Languages and Applied Linguistics at the University of California, Santa Cruz, and Director and Principal Investigator of the Bilingualism, Sociolinguistics, and Cognition (BiSoN) Lab. My research examines bilingual language processing and use through an integrated psycholinguistic and sociolinguistic perspective, with a particular focus on how bilinguals navigate and switch between languages in real-world communication.

About

I am an Assistant Professor of Languages and Applied Linguistics at the University of California, Santa Cruz, where I also direct the Bilingualism, Sociolinguistics, and Cognition (BiSoN) lab. I currently hold a Visiting Faculty appointment in the Department of Linguistics at Stanford University.

Prior to joining UCSC in July 2025, I was a Provostial Fellow and Lecturer in Linguistics at Stanford University. I completed my Ph.D. in East Asian Linguistics at the University of California, Los Angeles in June 2023, with a dissertation on code-switching in three groups of Cantonese-English bilinguals, supported by a Mellon/ACLS Dissertation Completion Fellowship and an NSF Doctoral Dissertation Research Improvement Award (BCS-2042479).

My scholarship intersects bilingualism, heritage and second language acquisition, psycholinguistics, sociolinguistics, and interactional linguistics — with a focus on how language experience and cultural identity shape bilingual language processing and code-switching.

Beyond the Lab

Born and raised in Hong Kong, I grew up speaking Cantonese, English, and Mandarin — part of what first drew me to studying bilingualism and code-switching as a researcher, not just living it. I enjoy bringing that work outside the classroom and lab, through public talks like "Save Cantonese" and seminars for admitted students on what happens in the brain when you speak more than one language.
